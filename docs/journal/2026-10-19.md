### Implemented

- **C1, one constant helper.** `rephrase.rs::push_mov_imm64` is the only constant builder: `movz` of the lowest non-zero halfword (with its `hw`), a `movk` for every higher non-zero halfword, `movz rd, #0` for 0.
  Callers: ADR/ADRP, BL/BLR link writes, the BL target move, every exit payload (status, param0, param1), and reg-virt's `push_mov_address` (literal loads), which is now a thin adaptor over it (reg-virt kind, error mapping).
  The 4-word `movz hw3; movk; movk; movk` chain is gone. Layout (`target_moves < 4`) and the verifier (rule 10, `link_target_of`) already accepted one to four words.
- **Slot forwarding post-pass** (`reg_virt.rs::forward_slots`, over every block *body* after `virtualize_insns`; the cold region is not processed: its exit groups hold no repeated load). Deletion only (a `keep` flag per word).
  - A fill/spill is exactly what `emit_fills`/`emit_spills` produce: kind `RegVirtHelper`, 64-bit `ldr`/`str xS, [sp, #slot]`, `S` in x12..x15, `slot` a stack-backed register's slot (x12..x17, x18).
    The pt_regs pointer load (`[sp, #176]`), the template's table load and the capture loads are not slot moves.
  - Pass 1 deletes a fill when `xS` holds `slot`: set by a fill or by `str xS`; cleared by any write to `xS` (`writes_physical_reg` over the generated operand roles; a role whose register the metadata cannot name counts as a write
    to every register), by another register's spill of the slot, and entirely at a join point or at a memory access that is not a fill/spill/user access/window access. User and window accesses only clear their destination registers.
  - Pass 2 (backwards) deletes a spill when a later spill of the same slot follows in the run. Ends of a run: any branch (`ControlFlow`/`BranchTarget` role), any memory access (user, window, other), an exit payload/branch, a fill of that
    slot (a fill deleted by pass 1 is not one), a join point, the block end.
  - Join points inside a body: the first word and the word after an unconditional `b`/`br` (the template's victim probe and the exit group behind it). Everything else the contract lists is a block start or lives in the cold region.
    User conditional branches end a CFG block, so the only branches inside a body are the budget, alignment, range and dispatch checks.
  - The ADRP into stack-backed x16 is `movz x12; movk x12; movk x12; str x12, [sp, #48]` (4 words for 11; unit test and `adrp_stack_backed_mark`).
- **C10.** `plan_window`: for a base-only access (offset 0, no index) `sA` is the register the base lives in: a stack-backed base's fill scratch, else its physical register (direct, x16 for x29, x17 for SP). No `mov sA, <base>`.
  `sB` is now the first free scratch, so its number changes in every window. An XZR base (never decoded: encoding 31 of these operands is SP) is `UnsupportedSpOperand`, an intrinsic rejection (native execution is exact).
- **Audit fix (before commit).** The dead-spill pass also ends a run at a link slot: the slot is a `nop` in the pass's input, but the runtime may patch it into a `b` to another fragment (A16), which then reads the slots.
  With the current lowering no spill can sit on both sides of a slot (the budget `cbz` precedes it, the template's table load follows it), so the output does not change: `make harness-test` after the fix passed 344, with the K0 golden byte-equal
  (**verified**). The gate and measurement runs below are from the tree before this one-line change.
- **C2 not implemented.** Precondition not met: I could not retrieve the Arm ARM clause that a load which takes a synchronous Data Abort leaves its destination registers UNKNOWN. Tried: WebSearch and WebFetch for DDI 0487 text; the
  developer.arm.com / support.arm.com pages are script-rendered (empty to the fetcher), the documentation-service PDF the search returned is the 1 MB "Known issues in Issue L.b" document (compressed streams unreadable), and the full PDF
  needs a download I may not make. Nothing was cited from memory. The contract says: without the clause this change does not ship. The SP-based `ldp` pair keeps loading its first register into scratch and a `mov` follows.
  - `kjit_can_run` does not exclude hardware watchpoints on a non-ptraced task: it declines `current->ptrace`, `_TIF_SINGLESTEP` and the work bits of `KJIT_BAIL_FLAGS`; nothing reads `thread.debug` / perf breakpoints
    (`kjit_glue.c` 1608-1621; `docs/pipeline.md` lists "Perf hardware breakpoints/watchpoints on a non-ptraced task are not considered" as a known limit). So a perf-event watchpoint on the second address of a pair would trap
    after the first register was written. (Reading, not run.) Under ptrace (the only route for debugger watchpoints) the task is declined.

### Contract issues

1. **C2 is not shipped, so three places describe code that does not exist:** `docs/pipeline.md` section 4 "Hot-path word reduction (A21)", bullet "SP-based pair loads write their first register directly"; the sentence in "Memory form coverage (A7b)"
   ("A pair's first load targets its final register directly only when ... or when the base is SP and both accesses share one 16-byte granule (A21 ...)"); and the 22:58 entry's expectation (1.0k words, C2 238 of it). Either remove the bullet and the clause,
   or keep it as deferred with the missing precondition. If it ships later: the harness `Footprint` (fault injection on the second access of an SP pair) must allow the first register to hold its old or loaded value; today only SIMD&FP loads and stores are allowed that.
2. The contract's join-point list ("stub, direct-branch target, template miss target or exit-group start") is wider than a body needs: inside a body only the first word and the word after `b`/`br` are join points (stubs and exit groups of the
   cold region are not processed). Not wrong, but the implementation and its mutation analysis rest on the narrower statement; worth one sentence.
3. Nothing in the verifier rejected A21 output, and no contract text about rules 3/6/8/9/10 needed a change.

## 01:31 +0800 — A22 decided: run-wide SP alignment and budget only on cycles

Commit: 56bc781 (base; the contracts are in the commit that adds this entry)

Inputs:
- A21 (01:22): -781 hot words per SET request.
- With KPTI forced on, KJIT is now below off on unix PING_MBULK (-0.110 us, 12/12 boots) and unix GET (-0.059 us, 11/12). With KPTI off it still loses (+0.04..+0.09).
- The measured cost was 0.07-0.16 ns per word, not the assumed 0.02-0.05. So the word count is still the lever, and the next two census candidates are worth their contract changes.

Decision: A22 = census C4 + C5, implemented in parallel in two worktrees, then measured together after the merge.
- **C4: user SP 16-byte aligned for the whole run** (`docs/pipeline.md` section 4, "User SP is 16-byte aligned for a whole run (A22)"; section 8, Run conditions).
  - A new run condition `regs->sp % 16 == 0`.
  - Fragment SP writes either keep alignment (`add`/`sub` by multiples of 16, writeback included, `and` with the low 4 mask bits clear) or are guarded before the first access, exiting through `Mem` when the result is misaligned.
  - All SP alignment checks and the A20 `SpAlignment` fact are deleted.
  - Exact independent of `SCTLR_EL1.SA0`.
  - Census: 460 hot words per SET request.
- **C5: budget check only on back-edges on a cycle of the fragment's body graph** (section 4, Execution budget (A6), "Budgeted back-edges (A22)"; section 6, rule 6).
  - Transfers are sinks, since they are already charged.
  - Translator (cfg/rephrase/layout) and verifier each compute strongly connected components independently.
  - Census: 321 words.

Why both now:
- They are the two largest remaining candidates after A21: 780 words, about 7% of the A21 hot path.
- Neither needs a micro-architectural answer first, unlike C8.
- C6 (RET on x30) and C7 (no budget on forward BL) stay out. C6 adds a second template shape for 166 words; C7 weakens verifier independence.

Expected: about -780 hot words per SET request. At A21's measured 0.07-0.16 ns per word that is 0.05-0.12 us per request (**assumed**).

### Implemented

- **Reg-virt emits no SP alignment check.** `SpAlignment`, its `observe_writes`, `is_sp_adjust_by_multiple_of_16`, `MemLowering::sp_align_scratch` and `WindowLowering::sp_check` are deleted. The acquire/release and
  atomic 16-byte crossing checks (non-SP bases) are unchanged.
- **One classification, `reg_virt::sp_write(insn) -> {None, Preserving, Guarded}`**, over the user instruction as decoded; used by `RewritePlan::build` (the lowering), by `rephrase` (which instructions get a `Mem` stub) and by
  the harness oracle (below). Writes SP = a `RegWrite`/`RegReadWrite` role on a field that is SP (reg 31, SP mode).
  - Preserving: `add`/`sub sp, sp, #imm` (64-bit) with `imm << (12*sh)` a multiple of 16; `and sp, xN, #mask` (32 and 64-bit) with the low 4 bits of the decoded mask clear (`logical_imm_mask`, the pseudocode's
    `DecodeBitMasks`, checked against the interpreter's decoder over every valid immediate); a pre/post-index writeback by a constant that is a multiple of 16, GPR (`mem_shape`) or SIMD&FP (`fpsimd_mem`).
  - Guarded: every other write (`mov sp, xN`, `add sp, xN, #16`, register operands, odd immediates, the 32-bit `add`/`sub`, `orr`/`eor` immediates, SIMD&FP writeback by register).
- **`SpGuard { value: xS, check: xT }`.** The new value goes into xS instead of x17; `and xT, xS, #15; cbnz xT, <Mem stub>` (kind `AlignCheck`); `orr x17, xzr, xS` (the instruction's own kind, like the writeback it replaces).
  - ALU forms (`RewritePlan::sp_guard`): the SP write field maps to xS (width kept), then check, then commit. Scratch: sources + xS + xT, at most 4 (`add sp, x12, x13, uxtx` uses the whole pool).
  - GPR writeback (`Writeback::guard`): `add/sub xS, x17, #amount` first, check, address materialization, accesses, commit, spills. xT shares the address scratch or the first-load scratch (the check precedes their writes).
    Worst case 4: `ldp x12, x13, [sp, #-504]!` (two stack-backed, address, xS; xT = address).
  - SIMD&FP writeback (`WindowLowering::guard`): xS = x17 + amount (or `+ Xm`), check through sB (xT = sB, which the range check writes later), range check, access, commit.
- **`rephrase` emits the `Mem` stub for a guarded SP write** (`accesses_memory() || sp_write == Guarded`), so the `cbnz` has its target and a non-memory instruction leaves through it.
- **`kjit_can_run`** (`kjit_glue.c`): `if (regs->sp & 15) return false;`, after the restart-range test. Entry paths, read in `runtime/exec.rs`: `kjit_rs_after_syscall` calls `can_run` first; `run_chain` calls it before
  an in-kernel syscall (SVC exit) and before every chained entry (BL/BLR/BR/RET exits); the dispatch hit inside a fragment is not a runtime entry (SP is aligned by the invariant). `kjit_call_fragment{,_fpsimd}` has no other
  caller. No path skips it. There is no per-condition declined counter: every `can_run` failure counts the same `RunDeclined` (`SvcDeclined` at the SVC exit), so no new stat. There is no Rust mirror of the conditions
  (`runtime/exec.rs` only calls the FFI).
- **Harness.** `URuntimeHalt::RunDeclined { pc }`: `URuntimeCursor::new` refuses to start when the SP in `pt_regs` is not 16-byte aligned (no `prepare_entry_at`, PAN stays set, state untouched); that covers `URuntime::new`/`with_cache`,
  the steppers, the cached differential and the session runs. The session's `hook` declines before the lookup and the profile, as the kernel does. The native runners (`run_fragment`, `run_cached`) decline the same way.
  A chained entry (`ContinueAt`) at a misaligned SP is an error, not a decline: it can only come from a fragment that broke the invariant.
- **Oracle.** The original under a KJIT run stops before an instruction that would leave an aligned SP misaligned (`OriginalStepper::stop_on_sp_misalignment`, `HaltReason::SpMisaligned { pc, instance }`, state of before it; a dry run on a copy
  with no injected fault decides it, so a fault injection cannot hide it; a fault of the instruction itself still wins). `run_original` always uses it. The fragment must leave through `Mem` at that pc
  (`runtime_halt_matches_original`); the native original is capped at the same instance (`original_stop_cap`, `step_capped_branch` now also executes a capped non-branch in place). An instruction that misaligns SP without being
  classified as an SP write is a harness error. `FaultCause::SpAlignment` stays in the interpreter for original code started at a misaligned SP.

### Decisions beyond the contract

1. The 32-bit `add`/`sub wsp, wsp, #imm` is guarded, not preserving: the contract names `add`/`sub x17, x17, #imm` only. (Its result is zero-extended, so it also changes the high half.)
2. `and wsp, wN, #mask` is preserving like the 64-bit form.
3. The commit word has the kind of the instruction (`Original`), the value computation (`add xS, x17, ...`) is a `RegVirtHelper`; the check is `AlignCheck`.
4. Misaligned SP at a chained entry or an in-kernel syscall is a harness error, not a decline (above).
5. The oracle stops the original instead of letting it fault later: with a misaligned SP unreachable inside a run, the old "fault at the next SP access" is no longer an outcome of a run.

### Contract issues

1. The contract (section 4, "Not a verifier rule") says fixtures cover a run that the harness declines at a misaligned SP. The fixture harness cannot express an initial SP (the `_fpcr<HEX>_` symbol segment is the only initial-state knob), and a declined run has no original to compare with,
   so it is covered by unit tests (`URuntime`, cached run, session). If a fixture is wanted, the symbol convention needs an `_sp<HEX>_` segment and a declined-run expectation.
2. The contract lists `add`/`sub x17, x17, #imm` as preserving; it does not say whether the 32-bit forms and `and wsp` are. Here: 32-bit `add`/`sub` guarded, `and` preserving (decisions 1, 2). Worth one sentence.
3. "Exact: whether SCTLR_EL1.SA0 is set or not" holds for the guarded write only if userspace re-executes the instruction natively after the `Mem` exit; the kernel does (`pc = x11`). The following hook declines on `regs->sp & 15`, which the
   next syscall after the misaligned write meets: the rest of the process runs native until SP is aligned again. A program that keeps SP misaligned for long has KJIT off for that time. Not measured; the guest gates run no such program, as far as I know.
4. `docs/pipeline.md` section 8 (Run conditions) lists the condition; the declined counter is the shared `RunDeclined`. If the condition should be told apart in `debugfs` stats, that is a new stat (not added).

- A native server slowed by 0.07 / 0.21 us per request blocks 1.8 / 4.5 per 1000 less on GET at KPTI off (3.4 / 8.7 less at KPTI on): block rate follows the server's speed in this closed loop, whatever the code. The KPTI-on increase of the block rate with KJIT on is the same relation read the other way.
- **Equal-speed check, KPTI on (D-k1-b6 alone, 4 pairs).** GET: `on - off` is -0.053 us with +2.8 blocks/1000, `onD100 - off` +0.035 with -3.0; interpolated to equal CPU per request (t = 0.60) the block difference is -0.7 per 1000 (`onD300`: -1.2). PING: `on - off` -0.110 / +3.5, `onD300 - off` +0.096 / -8.5, interpolated -2.9. In those two cells the block excess disappears at equal speed: a response to speed, not a KJIT cost. **SET is not resolved:** in that boot `on - off` is +0.022 (pooled over boots -0.031) with +3.7 blocks, there is no sign change to interpolate at; the `onD` states of SET do reduce the blocks (-3.0 / -5.4), but a block excess of +3.7 with an `on` server that is not faster than `off` is not explained by speed. (At KPTI off the `onD` states of SET go the other way, +1.6 / +2.5 blocks and +7.3 / +14.7 wake IPIs per 1000: the clients start to sleep.)
- **Protocol-independent checks run:** (i) the injected-delay family above; (ii) the spinner states (no idle vCPU on CPU 0): the gap onS - offS is +0.033 / +0.044 / +0.049 at KPTI off (4 boots) and -0.049 / -0.028 / -0.096 at KPTI on (3 boots), the same sign and about the same size as without the spinner in the same boots (+0.042, +0.097, +0.051 and -0.063, -0.019, -0.110); (iii) pipelined cells (`-P 16`, `-P 4`; GET off 2.0 M requests/s with CPU 0 24% idle, SET off 10% idle, SET on 1.33 M requests/s): the gap there is +0.103 (GET -P 16) / +0.154 (SET -P 16) / +0.117 (GET -P 4), the code alone. `-c 60 --threads 3` does not saturate the server either (CPU 0 idle 21%, gap +0.156 with 5.7 more wake IPIs per 1000).
- **What an extra block costs.** The micro boots price a remote wake-up at 6.8-7.6 us to the waker (14.35 vs 6.70 us for a write that wakes nobody, M-k0-b1; 13.34 vs 6.56 in the earlier micro boot) and 8.5-8.8 us of CPU to the woken task; at 8.5 us the KPTI-on block excess would cost +0.027 (GET), +0.042 (SET), +0.034 (PING) us per request plus the IPIs (+0.016, +0.015, +0.009), +0.04..+0.06 in all (**assumed** unit costs). The dose-response gives no usable per-block price (0 and 11 us per block in the two KPTI-on GET estimates; the events that change with a block also change the event-loop cycles per request), so the in-situ price is **unknown**.
  At KPTI off the block rate falls with KJIT on (-3.0, -1.0, -3.7 per 1000), so the term is zero or negative there: about -0.026 (GET), +0.008 (SET), -0.029 (PING) with the IPIs, at 8.5 us per block (**assumed**).

Summary of the protocol: realized fraction of an injected delay 0.46 (range 0.12..2.21, 24 states). (1) At KPTI off the intrinsic sum of the decomposition is +0.13..+0.20 us per request with the hot-unit saving (+0.06..+0.1 with the in-situ saving) against +0.043..+0.081 measured, i.e. no more than half of the server-side cost reaches `sum_exec_runtime` if the hot-unit saving is right. (2) An A23 saving is expected at about half its intrinsic size (the statistics ablation: 0.034 realized of 0.068 predicted at KPTI off, 0.055 at KPTI on). (3) `sum_exec_runtime` per request is not a clean reading of a server-side change in this regime, which is why the A23 table gives intrinsic and realized sizes. At KPTI on the measured win (-0.07..-0.12 GET/PING) is larger than the intrinsic sum (-0.03), the opposite of a give-back; the decomposition does not explain that (A17's KPTI-on budget had the same sign of residual, +0.12..+0.20).

### Method

Protocol of the 11:43 entry: kjit-guest, 4 vCPUs, M1/HVF, `rodata=off` on both KPTI settings, redis-server 7.0.15 pinned to CPU 0 (unix socket and TCP port open), `redis-benchmark -s <sock> -t get|set|ping_mbulk -n 1000000 -c 50 -P 1 -d 1 --threads 2 -q` under `nojit` on CPUs 1-3, `chain_budget` 1024, on = `enable=1 auto=1`, off = `enable=0 auto=0` on the loaded module. States of a boot are paired inside (boot, cell, rep); the state order rotates by one per rep and by a boot offset; one unrecorded 100000-request run follows every state change and a 1000000-request warm-up every cell. Headline gap: the uninstrumented module built from the worktree at 879cf18 (md5 `839d58ae`, `llvm-objdump` of its code identical to the 11:43 `mod-a22` build, whose md5 differs by build path). Attribution: instrumented modules (`kjit_glue-experiment.diff`, 15:25 method ported: timers, `svcn`, `decl1/2`, amplifiers; new: `x_rep_tramp/call/run` on copies of `pt_regs` in the micro boots, hook phase timers `prep/chain/post`, `x_rep_link/clookup`, `x_delay`, `x_nostats`; dropped: the check-word NOPs and `emul`, A22 left almost no check words and `emul` was 0 at A17). Every knob is off at load; the instrumented `on - off` is close to the plain one (A boots GET +0.042, SET +0.057, PING +0.051; D boots +0.038 / +0.122 / +0.050; N boots +0.029 / +0.093 / +0.060; plain +0.048 / +0.078 / +0.043), SET being up to 0.04 higher.
Modules: `instr` (`9a11eefe`) A-k0-b1, A-k1-b1; `instr2` (`077411cf`, + chain timers, link amplifiers) A-k0-b2/b3, A-k1-b2, D boots; `instr3` (`54fa7465`, + `x_nostats`) N boots and the micro boot; `micro-k0-b0` ran an earlier build (`4a7f09b5`, no `x_delay`).
Boots (labels `at22-...`, own ports 13122 / 2355, one guest at a time, each started at host load1 3.0-4.0 with no other qemu): A-k0-b1..b3 and A-k1-b1..b2 (instrumented, 17-19 states, 3 cells, 2 reps), H-k0-b1..b2 and H-k1-b1 (plain, 4 cells, 8 reps), P-k0-b9 (plain, `-P 16` GET and SET, `-P 4` GET, 8 reps), D-k0-b4 and D-k1-b6 (delay states, 4 reps), N-k0-b7 and N-k1-b8 (statistics states, 4 reps), M-k0-b1 and micro-k0-b0 (micro). 13 attribution boots + 2 micro boots (M-k0-b1 with the final module, micro-k0-b0 with the earlier one). Not counted: the first attempt of A-k1-b2 hung before the first serial byte (host load1 rose to 7 during the hang) and was killed by me after 21 minutes (`hung-attempts/`); it ran again successfully. Smoke and debug boots (2 smoke, 2 `amprun` debug, 3 failed micro attempts: `svc_bench` refuses `decl1/2`; `amprun` under auto mode died with SIGILL, so the micro boot uses `auto=0`) are deleted.
The micro boot of KPTI on and a `-P 16` boot with KPTI on were dropped to stay near the 16-boot budget.

### Not verified

- **Tick-based native time.** The 15:25 method (off utime minus on utime) gives the native EL0 code as 0.70 / 0.76 / 0.64 us against fragment code of 0.72 / 0.85 / 0.56 (KPTI off), i.e. translated = native, but the `-P 16` gap says +0.10 / +0.15. Ticks that land in the masked tail of a kernel exit are counted as user time, which would overstate native time after each of ~2.2 syscalls per request; **assumed** to explain the difference (a P1-versus-P16 fit gives about 50 ns per return to EL0 that the pipelined code does not pay; the in-situ `svcn - on` is 143 / 212 / 51 ns per in-kernel syscall for GET / SET / PING against 101 hot, so it does not confirm it). Not isolated: no PMU in the guest.
- Line 1 at KPTI on is the hot unit plus the null-syscall difference, not measured; the in-situ `svcn - on` is 0.231 / 0.367 / 0.264, lower than 0.375 / 0.387 / 0.377. Line 5 was not measured with KPTI on, and PING_MBULK has no pipelined cell.
- The timers perturb heavily (`onT - on` +0.43..+0.56 us at KPTI off, with 17 more wake IPIs per 1000); their corrections use hot unit costs (CNTVCT read 12.5 ns, per-CPU update 4.2 ns). Hook logic and bracket are good to +-0.02, the phase split less. Only 3 / 2 boots (KPTI off / on), 2 reps each.
- The in-situ premium of the hook logic (66 ns per hook call against 26 hot) is not explained (cold code, FFI, stats lines, TLB: no PMU).
- Realized fractions of A23 items 2-6 are estimates (0.45 from the delay family; the statistics ablation realized 0.34-0.8 of its prediction). The sticky bracket was not implemented or ablated. The delay family has 1 boot and 4 reps per KPTI setting, the SET cell at KPTI off shows a realized fraction of 2.1-2.2 for `onD` (clients start to sleep: +7.3 / +14.7 wake IPIs per 1000), so the family is not a single linear response.
- The stock-kernel baseline (module not loaded) was not measured: `off` keeps the hook registered and pays SRCU and the indirect call on every syscall (0.02-0.03 us per request by the amplifier; 0.055 at A17), so KJIT on against a stock kernel would be about that much worse.
- Per-block price; whether the closed-loop give-back holds with other clients, `-d 1024`, `-c 1/10`, TCP, AOF, the debug kernel or real hardware; `ug3` (3 client threads) shows +0.156 and was not decomposed.
- Host activity beyond the load1 <= 4 gate at boot start was not controlled (load1 after a boot 3.2-6.4).
